Case 1: The Mixed-Up Habitat

Where in the World Is Bob the Alien?

Teaching purpose

A 20–30 minute detective adventure using the Grade 5 Ecosystems: matter and energy unit. It rehearses ideas from lessons 4–5 and 7–9: decomposition, plant inputs, matter pathways and food energy. It does not replace the unit's plant investigation, evidence argument or independent model assessment.

Students investigate four illustrated locations, collect clues, repair a science scanner and choose a destination. Wrong choices give feedback without losing lives. Allow partner talk and read clues aloud as needed.

Route: Garden HQ, Mexico → rainforest stop in Costa Rica → woodland stop in England → mangrove stop in Florida, USA. Characters, dialogue, travel events and the scanner are fictional. A country contains many habitats; the named stop, rather than the entire country, has the ecosystem described.

4. Bob's repair — all colours

  1. Air and water supply the main materials for plant growth; soil minerals contribute smaller amounts.
  2. Decomposers return materials to the surroundings. Matter does not vanish when leaves break down.
  3. Sunlight captured by plants is the original energy source in these food chains.

Teacher questions and acceptable responses

  • Why is sunlight not plant material? It supplies energy, while air and water supply matter.
  • What does an arrow mean? Accept clear descriptions of material movement or energy transfer appropriate to the specific model. “Next” alone is insufficient.
  • What is missing from the short matter pathway? Air, water and additional plant–animal–environment connections. RED is a scaffold, not a complete 5-LS2-1 model.
  • Does energy cycle back to the Sun? No. It is transferred, and some is released to the surroundings as heat.
  • Can a habitat clue alone identify a country? Usually not. Pupils should combine it with the geographical witness clue.
  • What would happen if decomposers were missing? Dead material would accumulate and material-return pathways would be disrupted. Accept age-appropriate explanations without molecular detail.

The energy chains are illustrative models of feeding, not complete food webs or claims that every bird eats grasshoppers. The map is schematic. Completion demonstrates success with these selected tasks; it does not establish full NGSS mastery.

Curriculum and sources

Supports aspects of NGSS 5-LS1-1, 5-LS2-1 and 5-PS3-1. An independent evidence-based argument for plant growth and a more complete matter model remain necessary.

Mangrove information: Everglades National Park: mangrove ecosystems. Original vector illustrations represent fictional scenes; they are not scientific photographs.
